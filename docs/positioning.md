# Positioning

## The sentence

> **Find people to build with.**

Everything else is a footnote. The footnote exists because the honest version of
the idea — *"help me find people I'd actually enjoy knowing"* — is heard as
"adult friend finder", and that mishearing is fatal to a product.

So the wedge is not friendship. The wedge is work.

## Why work is the right wedge

Strong friendships form around repeated interaction, shared goals, mutual trust,
and observed competence. Strong startups form out of exactly the same four
things. They are the same mechanism with different labels, which means the
*surface* can be the thing people can ask for out loud.

| Ask out loud | Sounds like | Actually is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Find me a co-founder" | legitimate, urgent | repeated interaction + shared goal |
| "Find a study partner for security certs" | normal, tactical | shared goal + scheduled contact |
| "Find an accountability partner" | normal | scheduled contact + mutual trust |
| "Find people I'd enjoy knowing" | ??? | the same thing, unsayable |

The bottom row is what everyone means. The top three are what they can say.

## The narrowing

Do **not** start with "meet anyone". Start with one sentence a stranger can act
on:

> ✅ **Find project partners.**
>
> ❌ Meet anyone.

Then pick a niche where the problem is already felt and already discussed in
public. Tandem's default is:

**AI builders and security learners.** People shipping a small tool or studying
applied detection/privacy/ML. Why this niche:

- they already work in public, so discovery is possible without surveillance
- they already form teams, so the behaviour is established
- "I want a co-founder" or "I want to study with someone" is a normal sentence
- the population is small enough to seed by hand and large enough to matter

Other niches that fit the same shape, and are on the roadmap rather than in
scope: marathon training blocks, language learners at a specific level, podcast
launch, independent researchers.

## What the user actually gets

Not "a match". The output of a good session is two or three names, each with a
reason you can argue with:

```
0.833  Jon   + You want a project partner and they offer it
             + Shares 1 interest: security
             + Similar time to give (6h and 6h per week)
             - 11.5h apart, almost no overlap
```

The value is in the last line. A match you can disagree with is a recommendation.
A match you cannot inspect is a slot machine.

## The pitch, three lengths

**Ten words**
> Find people to build with. Not a dating app.

**One sentence**
> Tandem matches you with two or three people who want to work on the same kind
> of project, at the same intensity, on the same schedule.

**One paragraph**
> Dating apps found partners, LinkedIn found jobs, Discord found communities. No
> one built the thing whose only job is finding people to *work* with, because
> the honest framing sounds like a dating app. Tandem narrows to "find a project
> partner": you post what you're building and how many hours you have, verify
> you're a person without handing over a phone number we keep, and get a handful
> of matches with reasons attached. Friendships happen later, or they don't.

## What to say when someone calls it a dating app

> It's for finding someone to build a thing with. If you want the version with no
> work involved, you want a different product and you already have twelve of
> them.

## Anti-positioning

Things Tandem deliberately is not, and should say so in the marketing, because
each one is a failure mode the category has already demonstrated:

- **Not a follower graph.** No follower counts, no likes, no feed to perform in.
- **Not a people search.** You cannot look anyone up by number or handle.
- **Not a cold outreach tool.** Invitations are public replies by a human, at
  human volume.
- **Not a friend-maximiser.** The goal is two or three people, not two hundred
  connections.
- **Not free-to-spam.** Verification is a hard gate on appearing in results.

## The one metric that matters early

Not signups. Not matches.

> **Did any pair agree on a first session and hold it?**

Ten pairs who met twice is a product. Ten thousand profiles and no meetings is a
landing page.
